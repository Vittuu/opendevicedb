# Content policy

OpenDeviceDB exists so people can keep using hardware they own. To stay useful and
legally sustainable, contributions must respect these rules.

## Accepted
- Protocol descriptions written by contributors (message formats, sequences, captures you made yourself).
- Original code (SDKs, drivers, replacement firmware) under an open license.
- Links to external projects, teardowns, FCC documents and vendor documentation.

## Refused
- Proprietary firmware images, vendor source code, extracted keys, certificates or credentials.
- Anything whose purpose is to defeat a regulatory safety or compliance function:
  drone Remote ID, geofencing / no-fly enforcement, radio power or frequency limits,
  vehicle emissions or safety systems, medical device safety interlocks,
  appliance safety interlocks (door locks, thermal cut-outs, microwave interlocks).
- Ways to access devices or accounts you do not own, or other people's data.
- Circumvention of DRM for the purpose of copyright infringement.

## Device requests
Requests ("wanted" issues) follow the same rules: a request whose goal is refused above
(e.g. removing geofencing, accessing devices you do not own) is labelled `policy` and closed
after review. Requests are public; do not put personal data in them.

## Legal basis
Reverse engineering for interoperability is protected in many jurisdictions
(e.g. EU Directive 2009/24/EC art. 6, US DMCA §1201(f) and the triennial exemptions).
This is not legal advice; contributors are responsible for complying with their local law.

## Takedowns
Report a problem by opening an issue labelled `policy`. Disputed content is hidden
pending review.
